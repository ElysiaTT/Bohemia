We often imagine “being ready” as a particular moment: we have read enough books, learned every tool, or finally found an uninterrupted stretch of time.

But beginning something may not require so much ceremony. It can be a short article, a piece of code that solves a small problem, or the first question written on an empty page.

## Open the door first

A project that has not yet begun can become perfect in our imagination. Every page should look beautiful, every opinion should be well considered, and every sentence should deserve to be read.

There is nothing wrong with these hopes. But if they prevent the first step, it is better to put them aside for a while.

> The first step does not have to be an answer. It only needs to point toward a second step.

Build the blog, then write down a small, honest thought. Allow it to be rough and incomplete. Allow your future self to come back and revise it.

## Give a goal a size you can act on

“Make a great blog” is too large. “Write a short explanation of one problem today” is just right.

Big goals offer direction; small actions get us moving. Separating them can help both do their job.

### Begin with a specific question

Instead of asking “What should I write?”, try asking:

- What is something I only just figured out today?
- Is there an explanation I wish I had found sooner?
- What have I read recently that changed my mind?

Specific questions naturally grow into specific things to say.

### Leave a clue for next time

Whenever you stop, write down one thing that remains unresolved. The next time you sit down, you will not have to face an entirely blank page again.

It might be only a title or a link to look up. A clue is easier to pick up again when it is small enough.

## Make keeping a record part of everyday life

Writing consistently does not have to mean publishing frequently. What matters is finding a rhythm that fits your life.

A short piece written with care is usually worth more than a publishing schedule you cannot sustain. When inspiration is absent, read. When there is no conclusion, record the question. When time is short, leave a fragment.

This place does not need to be busy every day. It only needs to be here when you want to return.

## Write in your own voice first

Bohemia takes its name from Queen’s *Bohemian Rhapsody*. This is a stage for different voices. You do not have to wait for an idea to be fully formed before writing the sentence you most want to say right now.

There is more to write and more to discover. We begin here.
